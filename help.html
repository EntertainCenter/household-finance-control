1. What This System Is For

Home Finance Management is not intended to be full accounting software.

Its purpose is to help the household answer a much more practical question:

How much money is genuinely safe to use after allowing for the bills and income that are coming?

A bank account can look healthy today while much of the money sitting there is already needed for bills that will clear next week.

The system therefore combines:

  • actual account balances;
  • upcoming bills;
  • scheduled income;
  • planned transfers;
  • the Bills Account safety floor;
  • and the amount currently available for everyday spending.
The goal is not perfect bookkeeping.

The goal is fewer financial surprises and less need to rely on borrowed money to finish an ordinary month.

2. The Simple Operating Method

1 Look at Dashboard

See what is in the accounts, what is coming soon and whether a transfer is currently recommended.

2 Spend From Spending

Normal household purchases should generally come from the Spending Account rather than the protected Bills Account.

3 Record Large Spending When Useful

If a large purchase would materially change what the Dashboard thinks is available, use Record Spending.

4 Check-In

Once or twice per week, synchronize the application with the real bank balances.

5 Transfer Carefully

Move money only when the real bank transfer is appropriate and the forecast supports it.

Most weeks:

Dashboard → spend normally → record only significant spending → Check-In → transfer if appropriate.

3. Bills, Spending & Savings

Bills Account

The Bills Account is protected household money. Regular income generally enters here and recurring obligations are paid from here.

A large Bills Account balance does not automatically mean that the money is available to spend.

Spending Account

This is the normal everyday-money account.

Groceries, gas, restaurants, household purchases and similar spending normally come from this account.

Savings

Savings is separate money for reserves, emergencies and future goals.

Basic rule:

Protect Bills first. Spend from Spending second.

4. Dashboard

Dashboard is the main decision screen.

Recommended Transfer Today

This is the amount the system currently believes can safely be moved while respecting the upcoming forecast and preferred Bills Account safety buffer.

A recommendation of $0 may be exactly the correct recommendation.

Spending Account

Shows the money currently recorded as available for normal household spending.

Bills Account

Shows the latest recorded Bills Account balance.

Forecast Low Point

Shows the lowest projected Bills Account balance during the forecast period.

This is one of the most important numbers in the system.

It helps answer whether today's apparently available money is actually needed later.

Next Scheduled Income

Shows the next known baseline income expected by the system.

Bills Due Next 7 Days

Shows the known obligations coming soon.

Items Needing Attention

Indicates overdue income or bills that have not yet been confirmed.

Last Check-In

Shows how recently the system was synchronized with the real bank.

5. Record Spending

Record Spending is optional.

It exists so that a large purchase can immediately update the application's account balance rather than waiting for the next Check-In.

When to Use It

Use Record Spending when a purchase is significant enough that leaving it unrecorded could make the Dashboard temporarily misleading.

Example: Large Grocery Trip

Suppose the Spending Account contains $1,300 and you purchase $875 of groceries.

Choose:

Record Spending → Spending Account → $875 → Description: Groceries → Category: Groceries

The application immediately changes the recorded Spending balance from $1,300 to $425.

When NOT to Use It

You do not need to enter every small purchase.

Examples that normally do not need individual entry:

  • a $12 lunch;
  • a small convenience-store purchase;
  • routine minor debit-card activity.

What Record Spending Does

  • reduces the selected account balance immediately;
  • records the purchase in the spending history;
  • writes the transaction to the Activity Log;
  • makes Dashboard calculations use the new balance.
Do not record the same purchase twice.

Check-In will later confirm the actual bank balance, but Record Spending has already reduced the application's balance.

Practical rule:

Record significant spending. Let Check-In absorb the ordinary small stuff.

6. Check-In

Check-In keeps Home Finance synchronized with the real bank.

Step 1 — Look at the Bank

Find the actual current balances for Bills, Spending and Savings.

Step 2 — Enter Actual Balances

Enter those balances exactly as shown by the bank.

Step 3 — Confirm Income

Mark income Received only when the money really appears.

Step 4 — Confirm Bills

Mark bills Paid / Cleared only when the payment really clears.

Step 5 — Save Check-In

The confirmed balances become the new starting point for Dashboard and Forecast.

Source-of-truth rule:

If the bank and Home Finance disagree, believe the bank and perform a Check-In.

Check-Ins, cleared bills and received income are also retained in the Activity Log.

7. Forecast

Forecast projects the Bills Account forward using known bills, income and planned transfers.

30 / 60 / 90 Days

Use the period selector to decide how far ahead you want to examine cash flow.

  • 30 days — immediate cash flow.
  • 60 days — medium-term pressure.
  • 90 days — broader recurring patterns.

Protected Floor

This is the preferred minimum safety cushion for the Bills Account.

Colour Meaning
Green Forecast remains above the desired safety level.
Yellow Account remains positive but the safety cushion is being used.
Red Forecast reaches zero or below and action is required.
Forecast is only as good as the information entered.

Wrong balances, dates or missing bills will produce a less useful forecast.

8. Transfers

Money can be moved between any two active accounts.

Examples:

  • Bills → Spending
  • Bills → Savings
  • Savings → Bills
  • Spending → Bills

Planned Transfer

A planned transfer records an intention to move money.

Completed Transfer

After the real bank transfer has occurred, open the transfer and choose Complete Transfer.

Completing the transfer:

  • reduces the From Account;
  • increases the To Account;
  • marks the transfer Completed;
  • records both sides in the Activity Log.
Never complete a transfer in Home Finance before the actual bank transfer has occurred.

9. Activity Log

The Activity Log is the chronological history of important financial actions recorded by Home Finance.

It may contain:

  • completed transfers;
  • Check-Ins;
  • income marked Received;
  • bills marked Paid/Cleared;
  • significant spending entered using Record Spending.
Think of this as the system audit trail.

If you wonder why an account balance changed, look here.

The Activity Log is not intended to duplicate your bank statement transaction by transaction.

10. Regular vs Extra Income

Regular Income

Regular Income is dependable household income used for normal planning.

Examples:

  • employment pay;
  • CPP;
  • dependable recurring household contributions.

Extra Income

Extra Income is outside the dependable baseline.

Examples:

  • overtime;
  • music income;
  • refunds;
  • one-time payments;
  • unexpected income.
Conservative planning rule:

Avoid depending on uncertain extra income before it actually arrives.

12. Adding & Editing Bills

Add a New Bill

Go to:

Manage → + Add New Bill

You can also open Manage Bills and choose + Add New Bill.

Important fields include:

  • Bill Name;
  • Category;
  • normal payment amount;
  • frequency;
  • next due date;
  • optional end date;
  • optional final payment amount;
  • Essential / Protected;
  • Autopay;
  • Active.

Next Due Date

Enter the next date on which the payment is expected.

This date matters because Forecast uses it to determine when the money will leave the Bills Account.

Essential / Protected

Use this for bills that belong in the protected household baseline rather than optional recurring spending.

When a Bill Ends

Do not normally delete the bill.

Mark it inactive so historical information remains available.

13. Adding & Editing Debts

Add a New Debt

Go to:

Manage → + Add New Debt

You can also choose + Add New Debt from Manage Debts.

Debt information can include:

  • debt name;
  • debt type;
  • current balance;
  • credit limit;
  • interest rate;
  • minimum or regular payment;
  • payment frequency;
  • next payment date;
  • expected payoff/end date;
  • final payment amount;
  • payoff priority.

Credit Limit

This is mainly useful for credit cards and lines of credit. It allows the system to calculate available credit.

Payoff Priority

Priority can eventually help identify which debt should receive extra money first.

When a Debt Is Paid Off

Set the balance appropriately and mark the debt inactive rather than deleting the record.

14. Recommended Weekly Routine

This system should not become a daily bookkeeping job.

  1. Open Dashboard.
  2. Review Recommended Transfer Today.
  3. Look at the Forecast Low Point.
  4. Spend normal household money from Spending.
  5. Use Record Spending for a significant purchase if the Dashboard should know about it immediately.
  6. Once or twice each week, perform a Check-In.
  7. Mark income Received only when it actually arrives.
  8. Mark bills Paid/Cleared only when they actually clear.
  9. If a transfer is appropriate, perform it at the bank.
  10. Complete the transfer in Home Finance.
  11. If something looks wrong or confusing, use Log Bug / Test Note immediately.
The regular process should take minutes, not hours.

15. What Do I Do When...?

We buy $850 of groceries

Pay from the Spending Account.

Because $850 is a significant purchase, use Record Spending so Dashboard knows immediately.

We spend $20 on lunch

Do nothing in Home Finance.

Let the next Check-In reconcile the Spending balance.

A new recurring bill starts

Go to:

Manage → + Add New Bill

We take on a new loan or credit card

Go to:

Manage → + Add New Debt

A bill permanently ends

Edit the bill and make it inactive instead of deleting it.

A debt is paid off

Update the balance and mark it inactive.

The bank balance differs from Home Finance

Perform a Check-In using the real bank balance.

Spending is getting low

Do not automatically refill it.

Return to Dashboard and see whether the forecast supports another transfer.

Something in the application doesn't make sense

Use Log Bug / Test Note immediately.

Do not redesign the system on the spot unless the problem prevents normal use.

16. Log Bug / Test Note

During the test period, Dashboard includes:

Log Bug / Test Note

Use this whenever you notice something worth remembering.

Note Types

Type Use It For
Bug Something appears technically wrong or does not work.
Confusing The system may be working, but you do not understand what it is telling you.
Improvement Idea Something that may make the system easier or more useful.
Worked Well Something the system handled particularly well.

What to Write

Give the shortest useful description you can.

Example:

Type: Confusing

Page: Dashboard

Description: Spending is low but recommended transfer is $0.

Details: Had a large grocery trip yesterday. Need to understand why Bills cannot refill Spending yet.
This is extremely useful during testing.

It allows us to improve the system based on actual use instead of trying to imagine every possible problem in advance.

17. Four-Week Test Period

The application is now far enough along that the most valuable next step is real-world use.

Test-period rule:

Do not add a new feature every time you encounter one unusual situation.

Log it first. See whether it happens again.

Week 1 — Accuracy

  • Are actual balances correct?
  • Are bill dates correct?
  • Are expected income dates correct?
  • Does Check-In behave properly?

Week 2 — Spending

  • Is the Spending Account useful?
  • Is the weekly target realistic?
  • Does Record Spending help with large purchases?
  • Are we tempted to refill Spending too quickly?

Week 3 — Forecast

  • Does Forecast predict real low points?
  • Are any recurring bills missing?
  • Is the protected floor sensible?
  • Are 30 / 60 / 90-day views useful?

Week 4 — Results

  • Do we understand our money better?
  • Are there fewer surprises?
  • Are spending decisions more deliberate?
  • Are we relying less on borrowed money?
  • Which logged bugs or ideas actually deserve changes?
The key question after four weeks:

Has this system made it easier to protect bills, control everyday spending and see trouble before we need the line of credit?

19. Important Rules

Rule 1: Never mark income Received before the money actually arrives.
Rule 2: Never mark a bill Paid/Cleared before it actually clears.
Rule 3: Never complete a transfer in Home Finance before the real bank transfer occurs.
Rule 4: Never assume all of the Bills Account balance is available spending money.
Rule 5: When Home Finance and the bank disagree, the bank wins. Perform a Check-In.
Rule 6: Do not record every tiny purchase.
Rule 7: Use Record Spending for significant purchases that should affect the Dashboard immediately.
Rule 8: Do not delete historical bills or debts simply because they end. Make them inactive.
Rule 9: During the test period, log problems and ideas before changing the system.
Rule 10: Keep this simple enough that you will actually continue using it.

20. Troubleshooting

Dashboard balance doesn't match the bank

Perform a Check-In.

A large purchase happened but Dashboard still shows the old Spending balance

Use Record Spending if it has not already been recorded, or perform a Check-In.

I accidentally entered the same large purchase twice

Do not enter another correction blindly. Log a Bug / Test Note and reconcile the balance through Check-In.

A bill still appears after it cleared

Open Check-In and mark the bill Paid/Cleared.

Income still appears after it arrived

Open Check-In and mark the income Received.

Forecast looks wrong

Check:

  • actual balances;
  • bill dates;
  • income dates;
  • inactive bills;
  • unconfirmed bills;
  • unconfirmed income;
  • old planned transfers;
  • missing end dates.

A completed transfer appears twice in Activity Log

This is expected. One entry shows money leaving one account; the second shows money entering the other.

Something is technically broken

Use:

Dashboard → Log Bug / Test Note → Bug

Include what happened and what you expected.

Something works but I don't understand it

Log it as:

Confusing

That may indicate that the explanation needs improvement rather than the calculation.

The simplest way to remember the entire system:

Keep the balances real. Keep bills and income accurate. Protect Bills. Spend from Spending. Record large purchases when useful. Check-In regularly. Let Forecast tell you what is actually safe.

During the test period:

If something surprises you, log it instead of immediately changing the system.
