1. What This System Is For

Home Finance Management is designed to answer a question that a normal bank balance cannot answer:

How much money is genuinely safe to use after protecting the household obligations that have not happened yet?

Seeing several thousand dollars in a bank account can create the impression that there is plenty of money available. But some of that money may already be required for mortgage payments, insurance, utilities, loans, credit-card repayment or other future obligations.

The system therefore combines actual balances with future bills, regular income, planned transactions, debt payments, protected cash and other known obligations.

This is not intended to be a strict zero-based budget.

The goal is not to account for every dollar or prevent normal spending. The goal is to make better decisions while gradually improving the household financial position.

The system should provide useful guidance without requiring constant bookkeeping.

2. The Basic Household Model

Bills Account

The Bills Account is the household operating account. Regular income generally enters here and scheduled household obligations are paid from here.

This account should not be treated as ordinary spending money.

Spending Account

The Spending Account is intended for ordinary household life and discretionary spending.

There is deliberately no requirement that a fixed amount such as $500 must be transferred every week. The appropriate amount can change depending on the household's position.

Savings

Savings is a real bank account used as a reserve for emergencies and future needs.

Envelopes

Envelopes allow part of a real account balance to be mentally assigned to a particular goal without opening another bank account.

Debts

Credit cards, lines of credit, mortgages, vehicle loans, financing and other amounts owing are stored separately as debts.

The system separates three different ideas:

Where the cash physically exists, what some of that cash has been reserved for, and what money the household owes.

3. Protected Bills Balance

The Protected Bills Balance is the amount of your own money you ultimately want the Bills Account to retain above $0.

Current target Protected Bills Balance = $1,500 above $0

This does not mean the household must reach that level immediately. The system can still be useful while the Bills Account is below the target.

The long-term objective is to move from relying on overdraft, through $0, and eventually build a real cash cushion.

Overdraft is not part of the protected balance.

Overdraft is borrowed money. The target is eventually $1,500 of household cash above zero.

The safe floor is a destination, not a punishment.

If the household falls below it because of a legitimate unexpected expense, Guidance can help identify the shortfall. The goal is then to rebuild gradually rather than immediately eliminating all discretionary spending.

4. Available Funds to Transfer

Available Funds to Transfer estimates how much could leave the Bills Account while still protecting future obligations and the desired Bills Account floor.

Available Funds to Transfer = Conservative Forecast Low Point minus Protected Bills Balance

This is a safety ceiling rather than a spending target.

A $0 result does not mean the household can never spend money.

It means the Bills Account does not currently have money above the full protected target according to the conservative forecast.

Guidance is intended to provide a more realistic view while the household is still building toward that target.

Groceries and other necessary household spending should not be confused with optional discretionary transfers.

5. Dashboard

The Dashboard is the main day-to-day overview.

It provides quick access to important balances, upcoming obligations and financial decision information.

Bills Account

Shows the currently recorded Bills Account balance.

Spending Account

Shows money currently recorded in the Spending Account.

Forecast Low Point

Shows the lowest projected Bills Account balance during the forecast period.

Available Funds to Transfer

Shows the maximum amount currently above the protected forecast requirement.

Record Spending

Allows a significant purchase to be recorded immediately against either an appropriate cash account or a purchase-capable debt such as a credit card.

Do not judge affordability from today's Bills Account balance alone.

Future bills and obligations matter just as much as the current balance.

6. Check-In

Check-In keeps Home Finance synchronized with reality.

Bank Accounts

Compare the balances stored in Home Finance with the actual balances shown by the bank and update them when necessary.

Debts and Credit Cards

Check-In can also be used to review and update current credit-card, line-of-credit and other debt balances.

Regularly seeing the actual balances helps keep the amount owed visible and prevents the system from gradually drifting away from reality.

Income and Bills

Mark income Received only after it actually arrives. Mark bills Paid / Cleared only after they actually clear.

The real financial institution is always the final source of truth.

Check-In exists so the app can be corrected easily rather than requiring perfect transaction entry.

7. Forecast

Forecast projects the Bills Account forward using scheduled income, bills and planned money movements.

Conservative Forecast

The conservative calculation is deliberately cautious. It protects known obligations while avoiding dependence on uncertain money.

It can include:

  • current Bills balance;
  • scheduled regular income;
  • scheduled bills;
  • planned transfers from Bills;
  • planned credit-card/debt payments from Bills.

It does not depend on extra income that has not actually arrived.

Forecast Colours

Colour Meaning
Green Bills remains above the Protected Bills Balance.
Yellow Bills remains above $0 but falls below the protected level.
Red The forecast reaches $0 or below.
Missing bill dates matter.

An active bill without a usable next due date cannot be forecast correctly.

8. Financial Guidance

Forecast shows the mathematics. Guidance is intended to explain what those numbers mean for the household.

Guidance answers a different question:

Where are we financially, what needs attention, and what is a realistic next step?

Guidance can identify situations such as:

  • a forecast below $0;
  • a Protected Bills Balance shortfall;
  • limited room for discretionary transfers;
  • Savings that may help reinforce Bills;
  • missing bill information;
  • a negative Spending Account balance;
  • incomplete debt information;
  • high-interest debt;
  • other conditions that deserve attention.

As the system develops, this is also the natural place to show the progress toward rebuilding the Bills Account safe floor if the household is below it.

Guidance does not move money automatically.

It is an adviser. The household still makes the decision.

9. Accounts

Accounts are not hardwired into the application. The Accounts page allows the household's financial structure to change over time.

Accounts can include:

  • chequing accounts;
  • savings accounts;
  • Bills accounts;
  • Spending accounts;
  • other real cash accounts added in the future.

Account information can include its role, current balance, protected floor and whether it should participate in various calculations.

The system can grow with the household.

A new bank account can be added without redesigning the application. An account that is no longer used can be made inactive rather than permanently deleting its history.

10. Savings Envelopes

Envelopes are virtual subdivisions of money that physically remains inside a real bank account.

Example

Savings bank balance: $2,000

Vacation envelope: $500

Car Repair envelope: $300

The bank still contains only $2,000. The envelopes simply identify that $800 of that money has particular purposes.

Envelope balances are not additional cash.

A $500 envelope inside a $2,000 Savings account does not mean the household has $2,500.

Allocating money to an envelope

Allocating money assigns part of an account balance to the envelope. It does not create a second copy of the money.

Releasing money from an envelope

Releasing money removes that assignment and makes the money available again within the underlying account.

This provides the convenience of having multiple savings buckets without requiring multiple physical bank accounts.

11. Transfers & Payments

Transfers records intentional movements of money.

Account to Account

Examples include:

  • Bills → Spending;
  • Bills → Savings;
  • Savings → Bills;
  • Spending → Savings.

Account and Envelope Movements

Transfers can also be used to allocate money to envelopes or release money from them.

Remember that an envelope movement is an internal assignment of cash, not new income or spending.

Debt Payments

Money can be transferred from an account to a credit card, line of credit or other debt.

Planned

A transaction can be saved before it actually happens. A planned transaction from Bills can then be recognized by the forecast.

Completed

Once the real transaction occurs, open it using View / Edit and complete it.

Never mark a transaction Completed before the real movement of money occurs.

12. Record Spending

Record Spending provides a quick way to enter a purchase that is significant enough to matter to the current financial picture.

A purchase can be funded from an eligible cash account or from an eligible purchase-capable debt such as a credit card.

Do not turn this into detailed bookkeeping.

There is no need to record every coffee, small debit purchase or individual household item.

Record Spending is useful when:

  • a meaningful purchase occurs;
  • groceries are charged to a credit card;
  • a large discretionary purchase occurs;
  • you want the financial position updated immediately.

Check-In remains available to correct balances later, so missing a small transaction is not a disaster.

13. Credit-Card Purchases

Credit-card purchases require different treatment from purchases paid directly from a bank account.

When a purchase is made on a credit card:

The purchase is the household expense. The credit-card balance increases, but no bank cash has left the household yet.

Example

Groceries purchased on a credit card: $850

The app records $850 of household spending.

The credit-card amount owing increases by $850.

Bills Account does not decrease at that moment because the bank has not yet paid the card.

Only debts that are marked as usable for purchases should normally appear as funding choices for Record Spending.

14. Card Obligations

A credit-card purchase creates an important timing problem: the cash is still sitting in the bank even though some of it will eventually be needed to repay the card.

Home Finance therefore creates a card repayment obligation when appropriate.

Card obligation = cash that has not left the bank yet, but has effectively been committed by a card purchase.
Example

$850 of groceries are charged to a credit card.

The bank may still show the same cash balance, but the household now has an $850 obligation associated with that card spending.

This prevents card-funded household spending from falsely making the household appear richer simply because the cash has not left the bank yet.

As credit-card payments are completed, the related open obligations can be reduced or closed.

15. Paying Credit Cards & Debts

A payment from a bank account to a credit card is different from the purchase that originally created the debt.

Credit-card purchase = Expense

Later credit-card payment = Debt repayment / cash movement
Do not count both as household expenses.

Doing so would double-count the same spending.

When a debt payment is completed

  • the source bank account decreases;
  • the amount owed on the debt decreases;
  • the transaction changes to Completed;
  • the activity history records what happened;
  • related card obligations can be reduced or satisfied.

This same transfer mechanism can be used for credit cards, lines of credit and other debts where appropriate.

16. Grocery Card Example

Groceries are a good example of why the credit-card purchase system exists.

If groceries are purchased on a rewards credit card, the purchase can be captured without pretending that cash already left the bank.

Example

Grocery purchase: $850

Payment method: grocery credit card

Result:

  • $850 of spending is recorded;
  • the credit-card obligation increases;
  • the card balance increases;
  • the Bills Account does not immediately decrease.

Later, when the card is paid

Use Transfers and create a Credit Card / Debt Payment from the appropriate bank account to the card.

The payment reduces the bank balance and the card balance.

The later card payment is not another grocery expense.

The grocery expense was already recorded when the groceries were purchased.

This allows groceries to be treated separately from ordinary discretionary weekly spending while still protecting the cash eventually required to pay the card.

17. Activity & Transaction History

Activity provides a running history of important financial events recorded by Home Finance.

Depending on the transaction, history can include:

  • account transfers;
  • envelope allocations and releases;
  • credit-card and debt payments;
  • recorded spending;
  • credit-card purchases;
  • Check-In adjustments;
  • income confirmations;
  • bill confirmations;
  • planned, completed and cancelled activity.

Transaction history is particularly useful for bills and debts because it allows you to see what happened rather than relying only on today's balance.

If you wonder why a balance changed, transaction history should be one of the first places you look.

18. Regular vs Extra Income

Regular Income

Regular income is dependable or reasonably predictable income that can be used in baseline planning.

Extra Income

Extra income includes money such as:

  • overtime;
  • music income;
  • refunds;
  • unexpected payments;
  • other one-time income.
Conservative rule:

Do not rely on uncertain extra income before it actually arrives.

Once extra money actually arrives, it can help rebuild the Bills Account floor, increase Savings, support discretionary spending or accelerate debt repayment.

19. Managing Debts

Debts are not hardwired into the application. New debts can be added and old ones can be made inactive when they are no longer relevant.

Debt types can include:

  • credit cards;
  • lines of credit;
  • mortgages;
  • vehicle loans;
  • consolidation loans;
  • store financing;
  • other financing.

Useful debt information can include:

  • current balance;
  • credit limit;
  • interest rate;
  • minimum or regular payment;
  • payment frequency;
  • next payment date;
  • end date;
  • priority;
  • whether it can be used for purchases;
  • notes.
Keep balances and interest rates reasonably current.

This information will become increasingly useful for future debt-reduction guidance.

A purchase-capable card may sometimes have a negative balance. This represents prepaid credit rather than money owed.

20. Manage & Setup

Manage is used when the household's financial structure changes.

It provides access to areas such as:

  • Accounts;
  • Bills;
  • Regular Income;
  • Extra Income;
  • Debts;
  • and related setup functions.

Use Manage when:

  • a new bank account is opened;
  • an old account is no longer used;
  • a new bill is added;
  • a bill ends;
  • a payment amount changes;
  • a new credit card is added;
  • a loan is paid off;
  • an income source changes;
  • debt information needs updating.
Avoid deleting useful history.

Where possible, make old accounts, bills or debts inactive rather than removing records that may be referenced by historical transactions.

21. Recommended Routine

Home Finance should not require constant attention.

Once or twice per week

  1. Look at the real bank-account balances.
  2. Look at the current credit-card/debt balances when appropriate.
  3. Open Check-In.
  4. Correct account and debt balances that have changed.
  5. Confirm income that actually arrived.
  6. Confirm bills that actually cleared.
  7. Save the Check-In.
  8. Return to Dashboard.
  9. Review the Bills Account and Spending Account.
  10. Review Available Funds to Transfer.
  11. Look at the Forecast Low Point.
  12. Open Guidance if the financial position needs explanation.

During the week

You do not need to record every purchase.

Use Record Spending when a purchase is significant enough that you want it reflected immediately.

Credit-card purchases that create meaningful household obligations are particularly useful to record.

When moving money

  1. Decide what movement you actually want to make.
  2. Use Forecast or Guidance when the decision could affect Bills.
  3. Record it as Planned if it has not happened yet.
  4. Perform the real bank or card transaction.
  5. Mark it Completed afterward.
The routine should take minutes, not hours.

The system is successful if it gives the household better information without becoming a burden to maintain.

22. Bug & Test Notes

During the testing period, use the Log Bug / Test Note feature whenever something deserves attention.

This includes:

  • something that does not work;
  • a number that looks wrong;
  • a page that is confusing;
  • a feature that takes too many steps;
  • an improvement idea;
  • something that works especially well;
  • a situation the system does not seem to handle.
Record the thought when it happens.

Real household use is now more valuable than adding features simply because we can.

After a period of normal use, these notes can be reviewed together to decide which improvements are actually worthwhile.

23. Using It on a Phone

Home Finance is designed to work on phones as well as computers.

The main pages are mobile-friendly and can be used for quick Check-Ins, transfers, spending entries and financial reviews.

The Home Finance icon can be added to the phone Home Screen, making the website behave more like an installed app.

A useful phone workflow:

Dashboard → Check-In when needed → Record Spending or Transfers when something important happens → Guidance or Forecast when making a larger decision.

24. Important Rules

Rule 1

Never mark income Received before it actually arrives.

Rule 2

Never mark a bill Paid / Cleared before it actually clears.

Rule 3

Never mark a transfer or debt payment Completed before the real transaction occurs.

Rule 4

Do not treat the Bills Account balance as freely spendable money.

Rule 5

Do not count overdraft as available household cash.

Rule 6

When the app and the financial institution disagree, use Check-In to correct the app.

Rule 7

Do not try to record every small household purchase.

Rule 8

A planned payment from Bills is already reserved by the forecast. Do not mentally spend it again.

Rule 9

Available Funds to Transfer is a maximum safety figure, not an instruction to spend or transfer all of it.

Rule 10

A credit-card purchase is the expense. Paying the credit card later is not a second expense.

Rule 11

Money assigned to an envelope is still part of the underlying real account. Never count it twice.

Rule 12

Keep the system simple enough that you will actually use it.

25. Troubleshooting

Dashboard balance does not match the bank

Perform a Check-In using the actual balance.

Credit-card balance does not match the card provider

Update the debt balance during Check-In.

Forecast and Guidance do not agree

Refresh both pages. If they remain inconsistent, use Log Bug / Test Note.

Forecast looks too low

Check for:

  • planned transactions that should be cancelled;
  • wrong bill dates;
  • duplicate bills;
  • incorrect amounts;
  • old bills still marked Active.

Forecast looks too high

Look for missing bills, missing due dates or obligations that have not been properly represented.

A planned transaction is no longer needed

Open Transfers → View / Edit → Cancel Transaction.

Completed payment is still being treated as Planned

Confirm the transaction status is Completed.

A credit card shows a negative balance

This may be correct. A negative balance can represent prepaid credit.

A debt does not appear as a spending method

Confirm that the debt is Active and is marked as usable for purchases.

A debt is missing from Transfers

Confirm that the debt record is Active.

A bill is excluded from Forecast

Check that the active bill has a valid next due date.

An envelope appears to increase total household cash

It should not. An envelope represents money already contained in a real account. If total household cash appears to increase merely because money was assigned to an envelope, record a Bug / Test Note.

A credit-card purchase appears to reduce the bank immediately

A card-funded purchase should normally increase the card debt rather than immediately reducing a bank balance. If both happen for the same purchase, record a Bug / Test Note.

Paying a card appears as another household expense

A card payment should be treated as repayment of the debt, not another copy of the original purchase expense. If the same spending appears to be counted twice, record a Bug / Test Note.

26. Glossary

Term Meaning
Bills Account Main household operating account used for regular income and scheduled obligations.
Spending Account Account intended for ordinary household and discretionary spending.
Savings Real cash reserve for emergencies and future goals.
Envelope Virtual allocation of money that remains physically inside a real bank account.
Protected Bills Balance Target amount of household-owned cash the system tries to preserve in Bills above $0.
Available Funds to Transfer Maximum amount that could leave Bills while the conservative forecast remains above the protected target.
Forecast Low Point Lowest projected Bills Account balance during the selected forecast.
Protected Balance Shortfall Amount required to bring the forecast low point back to the Protected Bills Balance.
Conservative Forecast Safety calculation that recognizes known obligations without relying heavily on uncertain future money.
Planned Transaction Future movement of money that has been recorded but has not yet actually occurred.
Completed Transaction Transaction that has actually occurred and has been applied to the appropriate recorded balances.
Debt / Card Payment Money leaving a cash account and being sent to reduce a credit card, LOC, loan or other debt.
Credit-Card Purchase Household purchase funded by a credit card. It creates an expense and increases the amount owed without immediately reducing bank cash.
Card Obligation Amount of cash effectively committed to repaying spending that has already been charged to a card.
Credit Balance Prepaid value on an eligible credit card or financing account rather than an amount currently owed.
Check-In Process of reconciling Home Finance with actual account and debt balances.
Activity Log Historical audit trail of important financial activity.
Guidance Adviser page that interprets the financial position and identifies warnings or possible actions.
Extra Income Income outside the dependable baseline such as overtime, music income, refunds or one-time payments.
The simplest way to remember the whole system:

Keep the balances reasonably accurate, protect future obligations, distinguish cash from debt, avoid counting anything twice, and use the system to make better decisions rather than trying to account for every penny.

During the test period:

Use the system normally. If something feels confusing, inconvenient, unnecessary or wrong, log it. We can improve the system based on real experience rather than adding complexity in advance.
