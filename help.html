1. What This System Does

Home Finance Management is designed to give the household better control over day-to-day cash flow without requiring every individual purchase to be categorized.

The system separates money needed for bills from money available for normal household spending.

The main question the system answers is:

How much money can safely be moved from the Bills Account to the Spending Account without creating a shortage before future income arrives?

It does this by combining:

  • actual bank account balances;
  • scheduled income dates;
  • bill due dates;
  • recurring payment schedules;
  • planned spending transfers;
  • a preferred Bills Account safety buffer;
  • and a forward cash-flow forecast.

2. How the System Works

The system is intentionally simpler than a traditional transaction-by-transaction budgeting system.

Bills Account

Income normally enters the Bills Account and automatic household obligations are paid from it.

This account should not normally be used for groceries, restaurants, convenience purchases or everyday household spending.

Spending Account

This is the account used for groceries, gas, household purchases, restaurants and other normal discretionary spending.

Rather than tracking every individual purchase, the system controls how much money is transferred into this account.

Savings

Savings is tracked separately and can be used for emergency reserves and future goals.

Basic principle:

Protect the Bills Account first. Spend from the Spending Account second.

3. Quick Start

For normal use, most people only need four screens.

1 Dashboard

Look here first. Read the current recommendation and see whether anything needs attention.

2 Check-In

Periodically update the actual bank balances and confirm bills or income that have cleared.

3 Forecast

Use Forecast when you want to understand why the system recommends transferring money or waiting.

4 Transfers

Record money intentionally moved from Bills to Spending.

Normal workflow:

Dashboard → Check-In when needed → Forecast if needed → Transfer only when appropriate.

4. Dashboard

The Dashboard is the main screen and should normally be the first place a user looks.

Recommended Transfer Today

This is the amount the system believes can safely be moved to the Spending Account while respecting the cash-flow forecast and preferred Bills Account buffer.

The recommendation may be:

  • $0 — wait before transferring;
  • a partial amount;
  • or enough to bring the Spending Account to the weekly target.

Spending Account

Shows the amount currently available for normal household spending.

Bills Account

Shows the actual balance last entered through Check-In or updated through the system.

Forecast Low Point

Shows the lowest projected Bills Account balance during the forecast period.

This is one of the most important numbers in the system.

Next Scheduled Income

Shows the next known baseline income event and its expected date.

Bills Due Next 7 Days

Displays the combined amount of known upcoming bills during the next seven days.

Items Needing Attention

Shows overdue income or bills that have not been confirmed.

Last Check-In

Shows when the actual bank balances were last confirmed.

Important:

A large Bills Account balance does not automatically mean that money is available to spend. Future bills may already require most of it.

5. Weekly Check-In

Check-In keeps the system synchronized with reality.

Step 1 — Enter Actual Balances

Look at the actual bank accounts and enter the current:

  • Bills Account balance;
  • Spending Account balance;
  • Savings balance.

Click:

Save Check-In & Update Balances

Step 2 — Confirm Income

The Income Requiring Attention section shows income that is overdue or expected soon.

When the money actually appears in the bank, click:

Received

Confirm the actual amount received.

Do not mark income Received merely because it is scheduled. Wait until the money actually appears in the bank.

Step 3 — Confirm Bills

Bills Requiring Attention shows overdue bills and bills due soon.

Once a bill actually clears the bank account, click:

Paid / Cleared

Confirm the actual amount.

The system records the payment and advances the next due date automatically.

Important accounting rule:

Marking a bill Paid/Cleared does not subtract the money from the Bills Account again. The actual bank balance entered during Check-In already reflects the transaction.

6. Forecast

Forecast projects the Bills Account into the future using known income, bills and planned transfers.

Forecast Period

The user can view approximately:

  • 30 days;
  • 60 days;
  • 90 days.

Protected Floor

The protected floor is a preferred safety buffer.

Falling below the floor does not necessarily mean the bank account is empty. It means the household is using part of the desired safety cushion.

Forecast Colours

Colour Meaning
Green Bills Account is above the preferred safety level.
Yellow Bills Account remains positive but falls below the preferred buffer.
Red Forecast reaches $0 or below. Action is required.

Cash-Flow Timeline

The timeline shows every scheduled income and bill in date order and displays the projected balance after each event.

Forecast is only as accurate as the information entered. Check-In regularly so balances and cleared items remain current.

7. Transfers

Transfers records intentional movement of money from the Bills Account to the Spending Account.

Planned Transfer

A planned transfer is money that may be moved in the future. Planned transfers are included in the forecast.

Completed Transfer

When a transfer is actually performed at the bank, open the planned transfer and choose:

Complete Transfer

The system will:

  • reduce the Bills Account balance;
  • increase the Spending Account balance;
  • mark the transfer Completed.
Never complete a transfer in the app unless the real bank transfer has also been made.

8. Manage & Setup

Manage is used less frequently than Dashboard, Check-In, Forecast and Transfers.

It contains setup and maintenance screens for:

  • Accounts;
  • Bills;
  • Regular Income;
  • Extra Income;
  • Debts.

These screens should normally only be used when something changes.

9. Managing Accounts

Manage Accounts stores the accounts used by the finance system.

Examples include:

  • Bills Account;
  • Spending Account;
  • Savings.

Actual balances are normally updated through Check-In rather than directly through Manage Accounts.

10. Managing Bills

Manage Bills contains recurring household obligations.

Each bill may contain:

  • name;
  • amount;
  • frequency;
  • next due date;
  • end date;
  • final payment amount;
  • active/inactive status;
  • notes.

End Dates

If a payment is known to end on a particular date, enter the end date. The forecast will stop generating payments after that point.

Inactive Bills

If a bill no longer exists, mark it inactive rather than deleting historical information.

11. Managing Regular Income

Regular Income stores normal household income sources.

Examples include:

  • employment pay;
  • CPP;
  • regular household contributions.

Regular income should represent dependable or reasonably predictable income.

Do not treat uncertain income as dependable baseline income. Use Extra Income instead.

12. Extra Income

Extra Income is used for money that is not part of the dependable household baseline.

Examples include:

  • overtime;
  • music income;
  • tax refunds;
  • one-time payments;
  • unexpected income.

The system may display expected extra income in a forecast, but conservative spending recommendations should not depend on uncertain money before it actually arrives.

13. Managing Debts

Manage Debts tracks household debt balances and payment information.

Examples include:

  • credit cards;
  • line of credit;
  • vehicle loans;
  • mortgage;
  • other loans.

Debt balances should be updated periodically so the dashboard remains useful as a household financial overview.

14. Recommended Routine

Most Days

Open the Dashboard.

If everything looks normal and no action is required, close the app.

Once or Twice Per Week

  1. Open Check-In.
  2. Enter actual bank balances.
  3. Mark income Received when appropriate.
  4. Mark bills Paid/Cleared when appropriate.
  5. Save the Check-In.
  6. Return to Dashboard.

Before Moving Spending Money

  1. Check Dashboard recommendation.
  2. If unsure, open Forecast.
  3. Confirm the Bills Account remains safe.
  4. Perform the real bank transfer.
  5. Record/complete it under Transfers.

When Something Changes

Use Manage to update:

  • a new bill;
  • a cancelled bill;
  • a payment amount;
  • a due date;
  • an income source;
  • a debt balance;
  • an end date.

15. Teaching a New User

A new user does not need to understand the database, Supabase, GitHub or Vercel.

Teach the operational system in this order.

Lesson 1 — Explain the Two-Account Idea

Bills money stays protected in the Bills Account.

Normal household spending comes from the Spending Account.

Lesson 2 — Dashboard

Show the user how to find:

  • Recommended Transfer;
  • Spending balance;
  • Bills balance;
  • Forecast low point;
  • next income;
  • items needing attention.

Lesson 3 — Check-In

Have the new user perform a practice Check-In.

Emphasize:

  • use actual bank balances;
  • do not guess;
  • only mark transactions cleared when they actually cleared.

Lesson 4 — Forecast

Explain that the Forecast is why a bank balance of several thousand dollars may not mean several thousand dollars is available to spend.

Lesson 5 — Transfers

Demonstrate that the real bank transfer happens first or at the same time as completing the transfer in the app.

A trained user should be able to answer four questions:
  1. What money is actually in the accounts?
  2. What bills are coming?
  3. What income is coming?
  4. How much can safely be moved to Spending?

16. Important Rules

Rule 1: Never mark income Received before the money actually arrives.
Rule 2: Never mark a bill Paid/Cleared before it actually clears.
Rule 3: Never complete a transfer in the app unless the actual bank transfer has occurred.
Rule 4: Do not assume the current Bills Account balance is available spending money.
Rule 5: When actual bank balances and the app disagree, the real bank balance is the source of truth. Perform a Check-In.
Rule 6: Keep the system simple. Do not try to record every coffee, grocery item or debit transaction individually.

17. Troubleshooting

Dashboard balance does not match the bank

Perform a Check-In using the actual bank balance.

A bill still appears after it was paid

Open Check-In and mark the bill Paid/Cleared.

Income is still showing as future income after it arrived

Open Check-In and mark the income Received.

Forecast shows a bill that no longer exists

Open Manage → Bills and mark the bill inactive.

A payment amount changed

Update the bill under Manage → Bills.

A payment will finish soon

Enter its end date under Manage → Bills.

Spending balance is wrong after a transfer

Verify the real bank balances and perform a Check-In.

Forecast looks unrealistic

Check for:

  • incorrect bill due dates;
  • old bills still active;
  • income not marked Received;
  • bills not marked Cleared;
  • outdated account balances;
  • missing end dates.

18. Glossary

Term Meaning
Baseline Income Dependable normal household income used for planning.
Bills Account Account used for income deposits and household obligations.
Spending Account Account used for normal day-to-day household purchases.
Protected Floor Preferred minimum safety balance maintained in the Bills Account.
Forecast Low Point Lowest projected Bills Account balance during the forecast period.
Recommended Transfer Amount currently recommended for moving from Bills to Spending.
Planned Transfer Future transfer included in the forecast but not yet completed.
Completed Transfer Transfer that has actually occurred and has updated account balances.
Check-In Process of synchronizing the system with actual bank balances and cleared transactions.
Extra Income Income outside the dependable household baseline, such as overtime, music income or one-time payments.
The simplest way to remember the whole system:

Keep the balances real, keep the upcoming bills accurate, and let the forecast decide how much is safe to spend.
