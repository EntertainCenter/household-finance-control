1. What This System Is For

Home Finance Management is designed to answer a question that a normal bank balance cannot answer:

How much money is genuinely free to use after protecting the bills that have not happened yet?

Seeing $3,000 or $4,000 in a bank account can create the impression that there is plenty of money available. But some of that money may already be required for a mortgage, insurance, loans, utilities or other bills that will be withdrawn before the next income arrives.

The system therefore looks at:

  • actual account balances;
  • future bill dates;
  • future regular income;
  • planned account transfers;
  • planned credit-card and debt payments;
  • the Protected Bills Balance;
  • and the lowest projected Bills Account balance.
The purpose is not to stop you from spending.

The purpose is to distinguish money that is already spoken for from money that is genuinely safe to use.

2. The Basic Household Model

Bills Account

The Bills Account is the household operating account.

Regular income generally enters here and mortgages, loans, insurance, utilities and other scheduled obligations are paid from here.

This account is not considered ordinary spending money.

Spending Account

The Spending Account is intended for ordinary household life: groceries, gas, household purchases, eating out and other discretionary spending.

Money can be transferred into Spending when the Bills forecast says it is safe to do so.

Savings

Savings is a separate reserve.

It can be used for emergencies, future goals or, when appropriate, to reinforce the Bills Account.

Think of the three accounts this way:

Bills protects obligations. Spending funds normal life. Savings protects the household.

3. Protected Bills Balance

The Protected Bills Balance is the amount of your own money you want the Bills Account to retain above $0.

Protected Bills Balance = $1,500 above $0

If your Protected Bills Balance is $1,500, the system tries to keep the conservative forecast from falling below $1,500.

Overdraft does not count.

A $1,500 overdraft does not mean your Protected Bills Balance is effectively $3,000.

The protection level is $1,500 of your own money above zero. Overdraft is emergency borrowing and is deliberately excluded from Available Funds to Transfer.

Example

Bills Account actual balance: $4,000

Forecast low point: $2,300

Protected Bills Balance: $1,500

The forecast has $800 of room above the protected level.

The Protected Bills Balance is not wasted money. It is the cushion that absorbs timing errors, forgotten expenses and normal household unpredictability before overdraft or the line of credit is needed.

4. Available Funds to Transfer

Available Funds to Transfer is one of the most important numbers in the system.

It represents the maximum amount that could leave the Bills Account today while the conservative forecast still remains at or above the Protected Bills Balance.

Available Funds to Transfer = Conservative Forecast Low Point minus Protected Bills Balance
Example

Lowest conservative forecast balance: $3,500

Protected Bills Balance: $1,500

Available Funds to Transfer: $2,000

That does not mean you should transfer $2,000.

You could transfer:

  • $500;
  • $200;
  • $1,000;
  • or nothing at all.

Any unused amount stays in Bills and can build over time.

The $500 weekly spending target does not cap this number.

The weekly target is a budgeting reference. Available Funds to Transfer answers a different question: how much money is safely above future Bills requirements.

If Available Funds to Transfer is $0:

Do not make discretionary transfers from Bills.

5. Dashboard

The Dashboard is your main daily decision screen.

Most of the time, this is the first page you should open.

Available Funds to Transfer

Shows how much money could safely leave Bills based on the conservative forecast.

Spending Account

Shows the amount currently recorded as available for ordinary household spending.

Bills Account

Shows the current recorded Bills balance and reminds you of the Protected Bills Balance.

Forecast Low Point

Shows the lowest Bills balance expected during the conservative forecast.

This is often more important than today's balance.

Next Scheduled Income

Shows the next expected regular income deposit.

Bills Due Next 7 Days

Shows how much is scheduled to leave for bills during the next week.

Items Needing Attention

Identifies overdue bill or income records.

Last Check-In

Shows when the app balances were last reconciled with the real bank.

Do not judge affordability from Bills Account Now.

Use Available Funds to Transfer and Forecast Low Point instead.

6. Check-In

Check-In keeps Home Finance synchronized with the real bank.

Enter Actual Balances

Look at the actual bank and enter the real balances for Bills, Spending and Savings.

The actual bank is always the final source of truth.

Confirm Income

Mark scheduled income Received only after it actually appears in the bank.

Confirm Bills

Mark bills Paid / Cleared only after they actually clear.

Important accounting rule

Confirming a bill or income event does not change the account balance again. The Check-In balance represents what the bank actually says.

Check-In adjustments are also written to the Activity Log.

7. Forecast

Forecast projects the Bills Account forward using actual bill dates, scheduled income, planned transfers and planned debt/card payments.

30, 60 or 90 Days

You can change the forecast period. The normal working view is 60 days.

Conservative Forecast

The conservative calculation deliberately distrusts money that has not actually arrived.

It counts:

  • current Bills balance;
  • scheduled regular income;
  • scheduled bills;
  • planned transfers out of Bills;
  • planned credit-card/debt payments from Bills.

It does not rely on:

  • unreceived extra income;
  • planned transfers into Bills that have not actually happened.

Forecast Colours

Colour Meaning
Green The Bills Account remains above the Protected Bills Balance.
Yellow Bills remain above $0 but fall below the protected level.
Red The forecast reaches $0 or below.

Protected Balance Shortfall

This tells you how much additional money would be required to keep the Bills forecast at or above the Protected Bills Balance.

Missing bill dates matter.

If an active bill has no Next Due Date, Forecast cannot include it. Forecast will warn you when this occurs.

8. Financial Guidance

Forecast shows the mathematics. Guidance explains what those numbers may mean.

Guidance tries to answer:

Is there anything we should be concerned about right now, and what should we consider doing about it?

Guidance can identify things such as:

  • a Bills Account projected to reach $0;
  • a Protected Bills Balance shortfall;
  • no money available to transfer;
  • Savings that could help reinforce Bills;
  • Savings that are not enough to solve the shortfall;
  • missing bill dates;
  • a negative Spending Account balance;
  • incomplete debt information;
  • the highest recorded debt interest rate;
  • prepaid credit-card balances.
Guidance does not move money.

It is an adviser only. You remain responsible for deciding whether to act.

Guidance and Forecast use the same conservative cash-flow logic. Their low point and Protected Balance Shortfall should agree.

9. Transfers & Payments

The Transfers page handles two different transaction types.

Account Transfer

Moves money between household accounts.

Examples:

  • Bills → Spending;
  • Bills → Savings;
  • Savings → Bills;
  • Spending → Savings.

Credit Card / Debt Payment

Records money leaving a bank account and being sent to a credit card, line of credit, loan or other debt.

Planned

Save a transaction as Planned before it actually occurs.

Planned money leaving Bills is reserved in the conservative forecast.

Completed

Complete the transaction only after the real bank transaction has occurred.

Never press Complete before the real transaction has happened.

10. Credit Cards & Debt Payments

Credit cards, lines of credit, loans and financing accounts are maintained as Debt accounts.

Normal positive balance

A positive balance means money is owed.

Jim Visa: $7,500

This means $7,500 is currently recorded as owing.

Negative credit-card balance

Credit cards and store-financing accounts may show a negative balance when they have been prepaid.

Paula Costco Mastercard: -$150

The app displays this as: $150 Credit

Negative balances are not normally allowed for loans, mortgages, vehicle loans or lines of credit.

Completing a debt payment

When a credit-card/debt payment is completed:

  • the source bank account balance decreases;
  • the debt balance decreases;
  • the transaction becomes Completed;
  • the payment appears in the Activity Log.

11. Grocery Card Example

A credit card can be used for groceries in order to collect rewards or points without confusing the household cash-flow system.

Example payday grocery routine

Transfer/payment from Bills to grocery credit card: $1,000.

Grocery store purchase: approximately $850.

Remaining prepaid amount: approximately $150 for the second grocery store or later grocery top-ups.

How to record it

  1. Open Transfers.
  2. Choose Credit Card / Debt Payment.
  3. Choose Bills Account as the source.
  4. Choose the grocery credit card as the destination.
  5. Enter the amount, for example $1,000.
  6. Complete the payment only after the actual $1,000 has moved.
Do not reduce Bills again for the grocery purchase itself.

The cash already left Bills when the credit-card payment was made.

Recording both the $1,000 card payment and the $850 grocery purchase against Bills would double-count the spending.

What about the remaining $150?

It remains as prepaid value on the credit card.

The debt account can therefore show a credit balance until future grocery purchases use it.

Off-week grocery top-ups

If another $100–$200 is needed later, treat that as another credit-card/debt payment only if additional cash is actually transferred to the card.

12. Activity Log

The Activity Log is the audit trail for important account activity.

It can contain:

  • completed account transfers;
  • credit-card/debt payments;
  • significant spending entries;
  • Check-In adjustments;
  • income confirmations;
  • bill-cleared confirmations.

Account transfers normally create two entries: one for money leaving one account and one for money entering the other.

A debt payment creates an entry for the money leaving the source bank account.

If you ever wonder why an account balance changed, check the Activity Log first.

13. Record Spending

The Dashboard includes Record Spending for purchases important enough that you want the app balance to change immediately.

This is optional.

Do not try to enter every coffee, debit purchase or grocery item.

The system is intentionally not designed to become a transaction-by-transaction bookkeeping system.

Record Spending is most useful when:

  • a large purchase occurs;
  • you want the Dashboard balance updated immediately;
  • the purchase is large enough to affect a decision.

Recording Spending immediately reduces the selected account.

Do not enter the same transaction twice.

14. Regular vs Extra Income

Regular Income

Regular income is dependable or reasonably predictable income that can be included in baseline planning.

Extra Income

Extra income includes money such as:

  • overtime;
  • music income;
  • refunds;
  • unexpected payments;
  • other one-time income.
Conservative rule:

Do not spend against uncertain extra income before it actually arrives.

15. Managing Debts

The Debts page stores credit cards, lines of credit, loans, mortgages and financing accounts.

Useful information includes:

  • current balance;
  • credit limit;
  • interest rate;
  • minimum or regular payment;
  • payment frequency;
  • next payment date;
  • end date;
  • priority;
  • notes.
Guidance becomes much more useful when debt balances and interest rates are accurate.

If a credit card is prepaid, its balance can be negative and the page will display that as a credit.

16. Manage & Setup

Manage is used when the financial structure changes.

It provides access to:

  • Accounts;
  • Bills;
  • Income;
  • Extra Income;
  • Debts.

Use Manage when:

  • a new bill is added;
  • a bill ends;
  • a payment amount changes;
  • a new credit card is added;
  • a loan is paid off;
  • an income source changes;
  • an account needs to be updated.

17. Recommended Routine

The system should not require constant attention.

Once or twice per week

  1. Open the actual bank accounts.
  2. Open Check-In.
  3. Enter the real Bills, Spending and Savings balances.
  4. Confirm income that actually arrived.
  5. Confirm bills that actually cleared.
  6. Save the Check-In.
  7. Return to Dashboard.
  8. Look at Available Funds to Transfer.
  9. Look at Forecast Low Point.
  10. Open Guidance if anything looks concerning or confusing.
  11. Open Forecast before making a large discretionary transfer.

When transferring money

  1. Decide how much you actually want to transfer.
  2. Make sure it does not exceed Available Funds to Transfer.
  3. Create the transaction as Planned if appropriate.
  4. Perform the actual bank/card transaction.
  5. Mark it Completed in Transfers.
The routine should take minutes, not hours.

18. Using It on a Phone

Home Finance is designed to work on phones as well as computers.

The main pages are mobile-friendly:

  • Dashboard;
  • Check-In;
  • Forecast;
  • Guidance;
  • Transfers;
  • Help.

Cards stack vertically on smaller screens and tables can be scrolled sideways where necessary.

The Home Finance icon can be added to the phone Home Screen, making the website behave more like an installed app.

Best phone workflow:

Open Home Finance → Dashboard → Check-In if needed → Guidance or Forecast if something requires attention.

19. Important Rules

Rule 1

Never mark income Received before it actually arrives.

Rule 2

Never mark a bill Paid / Cleared before it actually clears.

Rule 3

Never mark a transfer or debt payment Completed before the real transaction occurs.

Rule 4

Do not treat the Bills Account balance as available spending money.

Rule 5

Do not count overdraft as available money.

Rule 6

When the bank and the app disagree, use Check-In. The real bank balance is the source of truth.

Rule 7

Do not try to record every small household purchase.

Rule 8

A planned payment out of Bills is already reserved by Forecast. Do not mentally spend that money again.

Rule 9

Available Funds to Transfer is a maximum safe amount, not an instruction to transfer all of it.

Rule 10

Keep the system simple enough that you will actually use it.

20. Troubleshooting

Dashboard balance does not match the bank

Perform a Check-In using the actual bank balances.

Forecast and Guidance do not agree

Refresh both pages.

They should use the same conservative low-point logic. If they remain different, review planned transactions and report it using Log Bug / Test Note.

Forecast looks too low

Check for:

  • planned transactions that should be cancelled;
  • wrong bill dates;
  • duplicate bills;
  • incorrect amounts;
  • old bills still marked Active.

Forecast looks too high

Look for missing bills or bills without a Next Due Date.

A planned transfer is no longer needed

Open Transfers → View / Edit → Cancel Transaction.

Completed payment is still in Forecast

Confirm the transaction status is Completed rather than Planned.

Spending Account is below $0

Compare it with the actual bank balance and perform a Check-In.

A credit card shows a negative balance

This can be correct. A negative credit-card balance represents prepaid credit.

A debt is missing from Transfers

Confirm that the debt record is Active on the Debts page.

Guidance says debt information is incomplete

Enter current debt balances and interest rates.

A bill is excluded from Forecast

Give the active bill a valid Next Due Date.

21. Glossary

Term Meaning
Bills Account Main household account used for regular income and scheduled obligations.
Spending Account Account used for ordinary household spending.
Savings Reserve account for emergencies and future goals.
Protected Bills Balance Minimum amount of your own money the system tries to preserve in Bills above $0.
Available Funds to Transfer Maximum amount that could leave Bills today while the conservative forecast remains above the Protected Bills Balance.
Forecast Low Point Lowest projected Bills balance during the forecast.
Protected Balance Shortfall Amount required to bring the forecast low point back to the Protected Bills Balance.
Conservative Forecast Safety calculation that counts future obligations but avoids relying on uncertain money that has not arrived.
Planned Transfer Future movement between bank accounts that has not yet been completed.
Debt / Card Payment Money leaving a bank account and being sent to a credit card, LOC, loan or financing account.
Completed Transaction that has actually occurred and has been applied to recorded balances.
Credit Balance Prepaid value on a credit card or eligible financing account.
Check-In Process of reconciling Home Finance with the actual bank.
Activity Log Historical audit trail of important account activity.
Guidance Adviser page that interprets the forecast and identifies warnings and possible actions.
Extra Income Income outside the dependable baseline such as overtime, music income, refunds or one-time payments.
The simplest way to remember the whole system:

Keep the balances real, keep the future bills and income accurate, protect Bills first, and only move money when the forecast says there is genuinely room to do it.
