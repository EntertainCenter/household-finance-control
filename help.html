1. What This System Is For

Home Finance Management is designed to make household cash flow easier to understand without requiring every individual purchase to be categorized.

The system separates money needed for bills from money available for normal spending and looks ahead before recommending that money be moved.

The main question the system answers is:

How much money can safely be moved out of the Bills Account without creating a shortage before future income arrives?

It does this by combining:

  • actual account balances;
  • known bill due dates;
  • scheduled income dates;
  • planned transfers;
  • the preferred Bills Account safety buffer;
  • and a forward cash-flow forecast.

2. Getting Started

For normal use, most of the system can be operated from four screens: Dashboard, Check-In, Forecast and Transfers.

1 Dashboard

Look here first. The Dashboard tells you what the current balances are, what is coming next, and whether money can safely be moved.

2 Check-In

Enter the actual bank balances and confirm income or bills that have really cleared.

3 Forecast

Use Forecast when you want to understand why the system says transfer, wait, or be careful.

4 Transfers

Record planned transfers and complete them only after the real bank transfer has occurred.

Simple routine:

Dashboard → Check-In when needed → Forecast if unsure → transfer only when appropriate.

3. The Two-Account Method

Bills Account

This is the protected account. Normal income enters here and mortgages, loans, utilities and other recurring obligations are paid from here.

A large balance in the Bills Account does not automatically mean that money is available to spend.

Spending Account

This is the normal everyday account for groceries, gas, eating out, household purchases and other routine spending.

Instead of tracking every individual purchase, the system controls how much money is moved into this account.

Savings

Savings remains separate and can be used for emergency reserves and future goals.

Basic principle:

Protect the Bills Account first. Spend from the Spending Account second.

4. Dashboard

The Dashboard is the main screen and should normally be the first place you look.

Recommended Transfer Today

This is the amount the system believes can safely leave the Bills Account while still respecting the forecast and preferred buffer.

The weekly spending target is a target balance, not an automatic transfer amount.

Spending Account

Shows the amount currently available for normal household spending.

Bills Account

Shows the latest actual balance recorded in the system.

Forecast Low Point

Shows the lowest projected Bills Account balance during the forecast. This is one of the most important numbers in the entire system.

Next Scheduled Income

Shows the next known baseline income event and its expected date.

Bills Due Next 7 Days

Shows the total of known upcoming obligations in the next seven days.

Items Needing Attention

Shows overdue bills or income items that have not yet been confirmed.

Last Check-In

Shows when the actual bank balances were last synchronized with the system.

Important:

Do not make spending decisions from the Bills Account balance alone. Look at the recommendation and Forecast Low Point.

5. Check-In

Check-In keeps the system synchronized with the real bank accounts.

Step 1 — Enter Actual Balances

Look at the real bank accounts and enter the actual current balances for Bills, Spending and Savings.

Then press: Save Check-In & Update Balances

Step 2 — Confirm Income

Income Requiring Attention shows overdue income and income expected soon.

When the money actually appears in the bank, press: Received

Confirm the actual amount received.

Step 3 — Confirm Bills

Bills Requiring Attention shows overdue bills and bills due soon.

Once the bill actually appears as cleared at the bank, press: Paid / Cleared

Confirm the actual amount.

Important accounting rule:

Marking income Received or a bill Paid/Cleared does not change the bank balance again. Check-In is the source of truth for actual balances.

Check-Ins, income received and bills cleared are also written to the Activity Log so there is a permanent history of what happened.

6. Forecast

Forecast projects the Bills Account into the future using known bills, scheduled income and planned transfers.

Forecast Period

The forecast can be viewed for 30, 60 or 90 days.

Protected Floor

The protected floor is the preferred minimum safety buffer for the Bills Account.

Falling below the floor does not necessarily mean the bank account is empty. It means the desired safety cushion is being used.

Forecast Colours

Colour Meaning
Green Bills Account remains above the preferred safety level.
Yellow Bills Account remains positive but falls below the preferred buffer.
Red Forecast reaches $0 or below. Action is required.

Planned Transfers

Planned transfers out of the Bills Account are included conservatively in the forecast.

Planned transfers into the Bills Account can appear in the visible timeline, but the conservative recommendation does not rely on them until they are actually completed.

Extra Income

Expected extra income can be visible, but the conservative spending recommendation should not depend on uncertain income before it arrives.

Remember:

Forecast is only as accurate as the balances, bill dates and income dates entered into the system.

7. Transfers

Transfers can move money between any two active accounts.

Examples include:

  • Bills → Spending;
  • Bills → Savings;
  • Savings → Bills;
  • Spending → Bills.

Planned Transfer

A planned transfer represents money that is expected to move in the future. Planned transfers are included in the forecast when appropriate.

Completed Transfer

After the real bank transfer has been performed, open the planned transfer and press: Complete Transfer

Completing the transfer:

  • reduces the From Account balance;
  • increases the To Account balance;
  • marks the transfer Completed;
  • and writes both sides to the Activity Log.
Never complete a transfer in the app unless the actual bank transfer has also occurred.

8. Activity Log

The Activity Log is the permanent chronological history of important account activity.

It can contain:

  • completed transfers;
  • Check-Ins;
  • income marked Received;
  • bills marked Paid/Cleared;
  • and future balance adjustments.

For completed transfers, the log records both the money leaving one account and entering the other.

For Check-Ins, it records the previous balance and the newly confirmed balance.

Think of the Activity Log as the system's audit trail.

If you later wonder why a balance changed or where money moved, this is the first place to look.

9. Regular vs Extra Income

Regular Income

Regular Income is dependable or reasonably predictable household income.

Examples include:

  • employment pay;
  • CPP;
  • regular household contributions when they have a dependable schedule.

Extra Income

Extra Income is money outside the normal dependable baseline.

Examples include:

  • overtime;
  • music income;
  • refunds;
  • one-time payments;
  • unexpected income.
Conservative planning rule:

Do not depend on uncertain extra income until it actually arrives.

10. Manage & Setup

Manage is used when something changes rather than during normal day-to-day use.

It contains setup and maintenance for:

  • Accounts;
  • Bills;
  • Regular Income;
  • Extra Income;
  • Debts.

Use Manage when a bill changes, an income source changes, a payment ends, a new account is added, or debt information needs to be updated.

11. Recommended Weekly Routine

The system does not require constant attention.

  1. Open the Dashboard and look at the current recommendation.
  2. Once or twice per week, open Check-In.
  3. Enter the real bank balances.
  4. Mark income Received only when it actually arrived.
  5. Mark bills Paid/Cleared only when they actually cleared.
  6. Save the Check-In.
  7. Return to the Dashboard.
  8. If a transfer is recommended, review Forecast if you want more detail.
  9. Perform the real bank transfer.
  10. Record or complete the transfer in the Transfers screen.
The normal system should take only a few minutes once or twice a week.

12. Using It on a Phone

The system is designed to work on phones as well as computers.

The most important pages are mobile-friendly:

  • Dashboard;
  • Check-In;
  • Forecast;
  • Transfers;
  • Help.

On a phone, cards stack vertically and tables can be scrolled sideways when necessary.

The site can also be added to the phone's Home Screen using the custom Home Finance icon so it behaves more like an app.

Best phone workflow:

Open Home Finance from the Home Screen → Dashboard → Check-In if needed.

13. First 4 Weeks of Testing

The first few weeks should be treated as a real-world test of the system.

Use real balances, real bills and real income rather than entering fake transactions just to test the buttons.

During this period:

  • do not constantly change the weekly spending target;
  • correct bill dates if you discover they are wrong;
  • correct income dates if needed;
  • watch whether Forecast behaves the way real life behaves;
  • note anything confusing or inconvenient;
  • use the Activity Log to understand changes.
What we are really testing:

Can the household protect upcoming bills, control normal spending, maintain a safety buffer and reduce the need to rely on overdraft or the line of credit for ordinary monthly cash flow?

14. Teaching Another User

A new user does not need to understand Supabase, GitHub, Vercel or the underlying database.

Teach the operating system in this order:

Lesson 1 — The Two-Account Idea

Bills money is protected. Normal everyday spending comes from the Spending Account.

Lesson 2 — Dashboard

Show how to find the current balances, forecast low point, next income, bills coming soon and recommended transfer.

Lesson 3 — Check-In

Show how to enter the actual bank balances and confirm cleared income and bills.

Lesson 4 — Forecast

Explain why a large bank balance may already be needed for future bills.

Lesson 5 — Transfers

Explain planned versus completed transfers and demonstrate the rule that the real bank transfer must occur before it is completed in the app.

Lesson 6 — Activity Log

Show where to look if someone later wants to know what changed or where money moved.

A trained user should be able to answer four questions:
  1. What money is actually in the accounts?
  2. What bills are coming?
  3. What income is coming?
  4. How much can safely be moved or spent?

15. Important Rules

Rule 1: Never mark income Received before the money actually arrives.
Rule 2: Never mark a bill Paid/Cleared before it actually clears.
Rule 3: Never complete a transfer in the app unless the real bank transfer has occurred.
Rule 4: Do not assume the current Bills Account balance is available spending money.
Rule 5: When the bank and the app disagree, the real bank balance is the source of truth. Perform a Check-In.
Rule 6: Keep the system simple. Do not try to record every coffee, grocery item or debit transaction individually.
Rule 7: Use the Activity Log for important system-level changes, not every individual purchase made from the Spending Account.

16. Troubleshooting

Dashboard balance does not match the bank

Perform a Check-In using the actual bank balance.

A bill still appears after it was paid

Open Check-In and mark the bill Paid/Cleared after confirming it actually cleared.

Income still appears as expected after it arrived

Open Check-In and mark the income Received.

Forecast looks wrong

Check for:

  • outdated bank balances;
  • incorrect bill dates;
  • old bills still active;
  • income not marked Received;
  • bills not marked Cleared;
  • planned transfers that should be cancelled;
  • missing payment end dates.

A planned transfer is no longer needed

Open Transfers, edit the planned transfer and cancel it.

A completed transfer appears in the Activity Log twice

This is normal. One entry represents money leaving the From Account and the other represents money entering the To Account.

Activity Log amount does not match an account balance

Income Received and Bill Cleared entries record the event, but actual bank balances are synchronized through Check-In. The Check-In balance is the source of truth.

Spending Account is getting low

Return to the Dashboard. Do not automatically refill it. Follow the current recommendation and review Forecast if needed.

17. Glossary

Term Meaning
Baseline Income Dependable normal household income used for planning.
Bills Account Protected account used for income deposits and household obligations.
Spending Account Account used for normal day-to-day household purchases.
Protected Floor Preferred minimum safety balance maintained in the Bills Account.
Forecast Low Point Lowest projected Bills Account balance during the forecast period.
Recommended Transfer Amount currently recommended for moving out of Bills toward normal spending.
Planned Transfer Future transfer recorded in the system but not yet performed at the bank.
Completed Transfer Transfer that has actually occurred and updated both account balances.
Check-In Process of synchronizing the system with actual bank balances and cleared events.
Activity Log Permanent history of major system-level account activity.
Extra Income Income outside the dependable baseline, such as overtime, music income or a refund.
The simplest way to remember the whole system:

Keep the balances real, keep upcoming bills and income accurate, and let the forecast decide how much is safe to move or spend.
